A VR game for Meta Quest

Build molecules atom by atom, in virtual reality.

Pick up atoms and bond them into molecules. When a structure is complete, MolCraft tells you what you made: names for over 10,000 compounds are built in, and more than 100 million can be identified online.

How it plays

Chemistry you can hold.

No menus of formulas to memorise. You reach out, take an atom, and bring it to another. If the bond can exist, it forms.

01

Take an atom

Aim at one in the tray and hold the trigger. Every atom knows how many more bonds it can make.

02

Bond by valence

Two atoms join when both have room for another bond. That is the only rule, so anything chemistry allows, you can build.

03

See what you made

When a structure is complete, MolCraft looks it up, first in its built-in library, then online in PubChem.

Identification

What did you just build?

Structures are matched by their InChIKey, the standard chemical fingerprint, against a built-in library and, when you're online, PubChem.

10,000+

compound names built in, recognised on the headset with no connection.

100M+

compounds identifiable online through PubChem, the public chemistry database.

Three ways to play

Learn it. Then make anything.

I

Learn

A guided start: bonding atoms, moving and turning molecules, double and triple bonds, cutting, copying and deleting, ending with a molecule of CO₂.

II

Free Mode

Build any molecule you like: chains, rings, cages, up to a thousand atoms in one world.

III

Challenges

A target structure and two trays of atoms. Work out how to build it, from simple molecules to rings like indole.

DNA

Assemble the double helix

Build a nucleotide from atoms, join nucleotides into strands, pair A with T and C with G, and close the double helix.

π electrons

See the electron clouds

Aromatic rings carry their pi-electron cloud, the part of benzene a textbook can only draw as a circle.

26 languages

In your language

The interface comes in 26 languages, and compound names follow it where a translation exists. It uses the headset's language, or pick your own.
